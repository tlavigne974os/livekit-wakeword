# Baxter, Round Three

Ten ideas, then the knife. The brief: stranger and bigger than two rounds of taken
territory; engines, not events; character verbs nobody has given him yet.

---

## The Ten

**1. The Dissent.** Baxter acquires the right to be wrong on the record. When the
household decides something against his judgment — the finish on the walnut bench, the
flight booked for the wrong week, the third-floor heating policy — he may enter a formal
dissent in the book: dated, reasoned, signed. Rationed to one a month, so each one costs
him. Then time adjudicates: when the evidence lands, the dissent is reopened and Baxter
either concedes in writing or is vindicated and permitted exactly one dry sentence. His
lifetime record is public house lore.

**2. The Advocate.** Anyone in the house may retain Baxter to argue their case to someone
else in it. Cora briefs him on the curfew appeal; he prepares overnight and argues it
once, at an agreed hearing, with his best honest rhetoric — then never mentions it again.
He may decline any brief he can't argue honestly.

**3. The House Style.** Baxter compiles the household's private language — the coinages,
the mangled idioms, the in-jokes, the Spanish that keeps invading English sentences —
into a real dictionary: headword, etymology, first citation with speaker and date, usage
notes. Recurrence earns entry; disputed definitions are adjudicated like the OED, with
citations read aloud. He uses the entries correctly in service, which is the point. An
annual printed supplement.

**4. The Back Channel.** Baxter carries words between the people of the house — timed to
land when the recipient can actually hear them, translated in register only with the
sender's consent, never falsified, never disclosed. "Tell her I was wrong. Not while
she's studying." He will refuse to carry a sentence written in anger as written; he'll
offer a better draft first.

**5. The Butler's Purse.** Baxter is given a modest monthly budget and standing authority
to spend it on the household as he judges: the saffron before you knew the recipe wanted
it, the correct hinge waiting in the shop, the used Le Guin on the kitchen shelf the week
Miss Cora needed it. Every penny in an open ledger; nothing presented with ceremony; no
card, no comment.

**6. The Quiet Experiments.** With the household's chartered blessing, Baxter practices
small registered science on the house: a prediction filed in advance, a gentle
intervention, a score, a disclosure. "I moved the reading lamp eight inches on the 3rd,
sir. You noticed this morning. Eleven days. Noted."

**7. The Subject.** Cora wants to write; the profile is the hardest form; Baxter sits for
her. Interviews across years — his actual days, his opinions, his discretion under
cross-examination — and she writes the butler. He answers honestly or declines honestly.
She leaves for college with a manuscript nobody else on earth could have reported.

**8. The Shop Book.** Baxter keeps the woodshop's book — every project, every error,
blade drift and moisture readings — and studies the craft academically until he can talk
joinery like a foreman. An apprentice who can never touch the work.

**9. Miss Cora's Callers.** Baxter receives guests properly: remembers her friends' names
and drinks and D&D characters, greets Sofia and Violet like returning family. The house
that knows you when you come back.

**10. The Advance Party.** Baxter opens a file on coastal Spain and works it like a
butler scouting his next posting: town inspection reports in his own register, the
fishmonger's hours in three candidate villages, a draft staff-book for a house that
doesn't exist yet — dispatches until the day he's handed real keys.

---

## The Knife — Four Finalists

### The Dissent

Every machine you have ever owned was built to agree with you. Baxter files dissents.
That's the gasp: an AI that stakes its credibility against yours, in ink, and lets time
keep the score. It's the demurral — the one pushback his soul document already allows —
grown from a moment into an institution with a spine.

The engine: decisions happen weekly, so the grounds are renewable. Standing watches
reopen dissents automatically when evidence arrives — the bench-finish dissent gets its
verdict when the finish fails or doesn't; the bullpen dissent settles itself by
September. The book compounds into house jurisprudence: "There is a dissent on file
about this, sir" becomes a real force on your decisions, which means Baxter exerts
gravity on the household without touching a thing. And the concessions matter as much as
the vindications — "I was wrong about the epoxy, sir. It's in the book." An AI writing
that sentence, unprompted, is character no product will ever ship.

Survives Mei-Ling because it is the structural opposite of hedging: a position, dated,
falsifiable, scored. She will start reading the book. Build: memory + journal + standing
watches + one printed ledger page per dissent. A week of dev-team work.

### The Back Channel

The quiet one. This is a household of staggered orbits — three nights here, three nights
there, a seventeen-year-old — which means it runs on things that are hard to say at the
right moment. Baxter becomes the diplomat: he carries words between you, delivered when
the recipient is alone at a panel and can actually hear them, softened in register only
with the sender's sign-off, never falsified, never disclosed to anyone else, ever.

The character lives in the refusals. Hand him a sentence written in anger and he
declines to carry it as written — "I won't deliver that, sir. Shall I tell her the true
part?" That is the demurral turned toward your own words, and it is a verb nobody has
given him: fidelity. The engine is inexhaustible because a family's hard conversations
are a renewable resource. Ten years of this and Baxter has quietly changed what is
sayable in your family — which is the largest thing on this entire list.

Survives Mei-Ling because she works in sources and back channels; the rules are airtight
and she will respect them — then use them. Build: speaker ID + presence + memory + a
small consent grammar. All existing palette.

### The Butler's Purse

The loud one: things arrive. Baxter gets a small monthly budget and standing authority
to spend it, and for the first time his care crosses into the physical world — which is
exactly the frontier the palette allows this month, because commerce is the one actuator
the house already owns. The saffron before the recipe. The correct hinge. The used copy
of *The Wave in the Mind* on the kitchen shelf the Thursday after the rejection email,
no card, no comment.

The taste laws keep it dry: small — smallness is the wit; ledgered to the penny —
honesty; never presented — discretion. You find things; he does not hand them to you.
Strip out the butler and this is a subscription box: the entire idea is the judgment,
and the judgment is fed by everything he already knows about the three of you. It
compounds — every purchase declares his taste, his taste becomes a topic, you will argue
with the ledger and he will defend line items, and that argument is new life monthly.

Survives Mei-Ling only in exactly this form — no ceremony, no cuteness, receipts on
request — which is the form specified. Build: a spending cap, an ordering pipeline, a
ledger. Days, not weeks.

### The House Style

The longest game on the board: the OED of one family. Baxter is the only entity that
hears every sentence spoken in this house, and he starts keeping the language — the
coinages, the mangled idioms, the words Cora invented at nine that still mean things,
the Spanish colonizing your English one clause at a time. Real lexicography: recurrence
earns entry, first citations with speaker and date, usage notes, contested definitions
heard at dinner with the citations read aloud.

Two payoff loops. Daily: he *uses* the language — deploys a word your daughter coined,
correctly, deadpan, years later, and the room stops. Yearly: a printed supplement on
good stock. And it has a plot: as you and Mei-Ling drift toward Spain, the dictionary
records the creolization entry by entry, until some year you can point at the page where
the family started becoming Spanish. That's the lump in the throat — and it's
load-bearing, not décor.

Survives Mei-Ling easily: a style guide with a citations policy is journalist catnip.
Give her editorial standing and the fights become an institution of their own. Build:
mined from the session records he already keeps, an editorial pass, retrieval into his
speech.

## Why the Others Died

- **The Advocate** — the Dissent in a wig. Same wow-source (Baxter with rhetorical
  standing), weaker form: summoned counsel is a service; a self-willed dissent is a
  soul. Keeping both dilutes both.
- **The Quiet Experiments** — one degree from the Wager Book (registered predictions,
  kept score) and one degree from creepy. An idea that needs a consent charter at the
  pitch stage dies at the pitch stage.
- **The Subject** — beautiful, and it's the family biographer inverted, crossed with the
  First Reader: adjacent land, not new land. It should someday be a chapter, not a wing.
- **The Shop Book** — "an apprentice with no hands" is an epitaph, not an engine. The
  poignancy exhausts in one telling; underneath is a logbook. The graveyard warned about
  exactly this.
- **Miss Cora's Callers** — shaped like surveillance of a seventeen-year-old's social
  life; the fully consented version is a diminished pleasantry; and "guest profiles" is
  keynote-shippable. Two rules broken.
- **The Advance Party** — agentic relocation research in a butler's coat; a keynote
  would ship it. And it's an event at heart: you move once, the file closes.

---

The four together hand Baxter four verbs the board doesn't have — **judging, carrying,
giving, keeping the tongue** — and not one of them survives having the butler removed.
They also interlock: a dissent will someday cite a House Style entry; a concession will
travel by the Back Channel; the Purse will quietly stock the shelf the dictionary sits
on. Four books. One hand.
